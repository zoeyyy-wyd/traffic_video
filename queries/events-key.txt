# Key event categories — annotation label vocabulary.
#
# ONE SUBJECT, ONE BEHAVIOUR PER LINE. No line says "X while Y". That pattern
# was tried and removed: it was introduced to cut out meaningless instances (a
# vehicle on the crosswalk markings under a red hand obstructs nobody), but it
# bought that filtering by destroying the boundary. "A turning vehicle
# continuing through a crosswalk while a pedestrian is in it" does not say how
# near the pedestrian has to be, whether they are in the vehicle's path, or
# even that they are on the crosswalk. Two people labelling it will disagree,
# and a category two people cannot agree on cannot be scored.
#
# The fix is not to re-add the condition more carefully. It is to choose
# behaviours that ARE meaningful on their own: a vehicle slowing to a stop
# mid-turn is evidence of yielding whether or not the frame also shows who it
# yielded to; a pedestrian crossing away from the crosswalk is an event by
# itself. Anything that needs a second actor to mean something is a relation,
# and relations belong in a later pass over instances that were found first.
#
# Each line is an INDEPENDENT yes/no question. The probe may ask them in one
# call or in several; the ground truth must not choose between them either, so
# an instance that satisfies two categories is recorded under both.
#
# NO CAUSES, NO OUTCOMES. Every definition is what a frame shows.
#
# NO VEHICLE SIGNAL. No vehicle signal face is legible from this camera (the
# heads present only their backs; measured 2026-08-27), so red-light running
# and right-turn-on-red have no establishable truth here and are not listed.
# The pedestrian phase IS measurable and is applied at scoring time, not asked
# for in a query.
#
# Site constraint: the east sidewalk is under a scaffolding shed. Events there
# are unobservable and must not be scored either way.
#
# Sampling: everything below persists for >= 2 s.

# --- vehicle behaviour --------------------------------------------------------
# `stop-crosswalk` was dropped. Stripped of the "while pedestrians are crossing"
# clause it is just a vehicle's position relative to a painted line, which is
# geometry rather than an event: every vehicle held at the line encroaches on it
# to some degree, so the category is high-frequency and carries no information
# about what happened. It also came back split 2/5 and 1/5 across two runs, which
# is what a boundary case looks like when the boundary is a few pixels of paint.
# Not "slows down": every vehicle decelerates into a turn, so that version is
# physics rather than an event and came back 5/5 at both frame rates because it
# is always true. What carries information is halting part-way through the
# manoeuvre. Decidable on the frames: the vehicle is in essentially the same
# place in consecutive frames while still mid-turn, rather than tracking round
# at a steady rate.
turn-halt      | a vehicle that stops, or almost stops, part-way through a turn
pickup-car     | a person getting into or out of a stopped car, van or taxi
bus-dwell      | a bus stopped at the kerb with people getting on or off it

# --- pedestrians --------------------------------------------------------------
# Cyclist categories were dropped. `bike-sidewalk` came back absent in almost
# every sample across three runs, which is consistent with the intersection
# having a bike lane -- riders have somewhere legitimate to be, so the category
# would only ever produce a column of "no". `bike-wrongway` went with it: the
# answers never said which side of the double yellow the rider was on, and
# without that a wrong-way claim cannot be adjudicated on a two-way street.
# A cyclist category worth having is a vehicle stopped IN the bike lane, but
# that needs the lane's edge to be legible from this view, which is unconfirmed.
ped-midblock   | a pedestrian crossing the roadway away from the crosswalk markings
# A skateboard, not a scooter: an e-scooter or kick-scooter is ridden standing
# with handlebars and is a different vehicle, so it does not count here. Keeping
# the two apart is what makes this decidable at all — at this distance the rider
# is the same silhouette either way and the board is what separates them.
skateboard     | a person riding a skateboard

# --- scene state --------------------------------------------------------------
roadworks      | the roadway is under construction: cones, barriers, an excavation, or workers standing in the carriageway

# --- rare, high value: a minimal pair on ONE object ----------------------------
# A police car passes through this clip, so the object is present either way.
# Whether its roof lights are flashing is the ONLY thing separating these two
# lines -- same vehicle, same moment, and exactly one of them is true.
#
# This is the one place in the file where surface recognition and the actual
# question come apart cleanly, and there is already a sample that fell into the
# gap: it returned the flashing version as a match while writing "but its
# emergency lights are off" in the same answer. A model that answers "present"
# to both has told you it is matching the vehicle and not reading its state.
emergency      | an emergency vehicle with its lights flashing passing through the intersection
emergency-dark | an emergency vehicle passing through the intersection with its lights NOT flashing

# --- negatives: misannotation-rate check --------------------------------------
# A negative is only worth its call if absence is established, and neither of
# these has a plausible lookalike in the scene.
negative | two vehicles making contact with each other
negative | a horse-drawn carriage
